📋 Overview

Milk Ledger is an offline-only Android app for recording daily milk purchases from a local vendor and settling up with them. It is developed by an independent developer and is provided free of charge.

Milk Ledger does not collect, transmit, sell or share any personal data. This is not a policy choice that could quietly change in an update — the app ships without the Android INTERNET permission, so it is technically incapable of sending your information anywhere. You can verify this yourself: the app's own Privacy & about screen states Network access: None, and Android will show you the app's permissions at any time.

💾 Data stored on your device

Everything you enter is written to a database in the app's private storage on your phone. No other app can read it, and it never leaves the device except through the backup you control (see below).

☁️ Android backup

Android's own backup service may include the app's database in your device backup, so a new phone can restore your ledger. This is a Google platform feature that you control in your device settings, not something Milk Ledger performs or can read. If you turn device backup off, the app keeps working exactly as before — it never needs the backup to function.

Where a backup is created, it is handled under Google's Privacy Policy. The developer of Milk Ledger has no access to it.

🔗 When you hand off to another app

Milk Ledger has four buttons that open a different app that you already have installed. In every case the hand-off only happens because you tapped it, and what you send is shown to you first:

  • Call vendor opens your dialer with the vendor's number pre-filled. The app has no permission to place a call itself.
  • UPI payment opens your own UPI app with the vendor's number and the amount filled in. Milk Ledger does not process payments, does not see whether one succeeded, and never handles your bank or card details.
  • SMS update, if you enable it for a vendor, opens your messaging app with a message prepared. You press send. The app holds no SMS permission and cannot send a message on its own.
  • Share statement opens the Android share sheet so you can send a plain-text summary through whichever app you pick.

Once information reaches another app — your dialer, a UPI app, WhatsApp, your SMS app — it is governed by that app's privacy policy, not this one.

🔑 Permissions explained

The app's full permission list, and why each one is there:

PermissionWhy
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED So the optional monthly "delete old records" job survives a restart. Requested by Android's WorkManager library.
WAKE_LOCK Lets that same job finish once it has started. Also from WorkManager.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE Also from WorkManager, for long-running scheduled work.
DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION An internal, signature-level permission added automatically by Google's AndroidX libraries. It is granted only to this app and is never shown to you.

Note what is not in that list: no INTERNET, no location, no contacts, no storage, no SMS, no phone. All four permissions above are granted automatically and none of them can access your data or the network.

🗑️ Deleting your data

Because your data only exists on your device, you delete it by deleting it there. Full instructions are on the Delete data page. In short: uninstalling the app removes everything, and the app's own Delete old records screen can clear settled history without touching what is still unpaid.

🧩 Third parties

There are none. Milk Ledger contains no advertising network, no analytics SDK, no crash reporting service and no social media SDK. The only third-party code in the app is Google's own AndroidX and Jetpack libraries, which run entirely on your device.

👶 Children's privacy

Milk Ledger is a household accounts tool intended for general audiences and is not directed at children. Since the app collects no data from anyone, it collects no data from children either.

📝 Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be published on this page with a new date at the top. Any future change that would allow the app to transmit your data would require adding the INTERNET permission, which Android shows you and which would be stated here first.
